# Least squares 
If we need an answer, even when one does not exist, we minmize the distance of $b-A\vec{x}$. So we need to find the single $\hat{x}$ such that for all $\vec{x}$ in $A$:
$$
b-A\hat{x} \leq b-A\vec{x}
$$
In particular, we just project $\vec{b}$ onto $\text{Col } A$

We know that the difference between the projection and the actual vector is orthogonal to the columns of $A$
![[meta/images/Pasted image 20250407113659.png]]
$$
a_{j} \cdot (b - A\hat{x}) = 0
$$

which looks smth like:
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
a_{j_{1}} \\
a_{j_{2}} \\
a_{j_{3}}
\end{bmatrix} \cdot  
\begin{bmatrix}
z_{1} \\
z_{2} \\
z_{3}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

If we want to use matrix multiplication instead of inner product we have to transpose $a$.

$$
a_{j}^T(b - A\hat{x}) = 0
$$
And now that we have transposed it, we can just use the entire matri:
$$
\begin{align}
A^T(b-Ax)=0 \\
A^Tb-A^TAx=0 \\
A^Tb=A^TAx
\end{align}
$$
This final equation is called the system of normal equations for $A\vec{x}=\vec{b}$ and a solution is denoted as $\hat{x}$

Often we are solving for $\vec{x}$ in such a way that the inverse is easier to calculate (it is a 2x2 matrix), so:
$$
(A^TA)^{-1}A^T\vec{b}=\vec{x}
$$

$$
A^T=\begin{bmatrix}
4 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 2 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
A^TA=\begin{bmatrix}
4 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 2 & 1
\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}
4 & 0 \\
0 & 2 \\
1 & 1
\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
17 & 1 \\
1 & 5
\end{bmatrix}
$$
$$
A^Tb=\begin{bmatrix}
4 & 0 & 1 \\
0 & 2 & 1
\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}
2 \\
0 \\
11
\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
19 \\
11
\end{bmatrix}
$$
$$
(A^TA)^{-1} = \frac{1}{84}\begin{bmatrix}
5 & -1 \\
-1 & 17
\end{bmatrix}
$$
$$
(A^TA)^{-1}A^T\vec{b}= \frac{1}{84}\begin{bmatrix}
5 & -1 \\
-1 & 17
\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}
19 \\
11
\end{bmatrix} =\frac{1}{84} \begin{bmatrix}
95-11 \\
-19+187
\end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{84}\begin{bmatrix}
84 \\
168
\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}
1 \\
2
\end{bmatrix}
$$
