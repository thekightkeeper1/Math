To diagonalize  $A$ means to find $P$ and $D$ such that
$$
A=PDP^{-1}
$$
Notice how the inverse is on the end.
When you solve for $P$ and $D$ you can check this equation rather than computing an inverse:

$$
AP=PD
$$

- $D$ is made up of the eigenvalues of $A$ in a diagonal line. Its the diagonal matrix.
- $P$ Is made up of **linearly independent** eigenvectors of $A$.
- If they aren't all LI (because there arent enough LI) then it is not diagonalizable
- Note that if Amxn has $n$ eigenvalues, then it 100% is diagonalizable 
	- However you could have # eigenvalues $<n$ but still have enough eigenvectors
	- Thats cause you do $A-\lambda I$ and find that null space. It might yield multiple basis vectors, and each of those is a LI eigenvector
