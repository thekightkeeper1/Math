# Orthogonal basis
To calculate $\left[\mathbf{y}\right]_{\mathcal{W}}$ given $\vec{y}$ and an orthogonal set $U$ as a basis to $W$:
$$
c_{j} = \frac{y \cdot u_{j}}{\left| \left| u_{j} \right| \right| ^2}
$$

![[meta/images/Pasted image 20250329134132.png]]
![[meta/images/Pasted image 20250331085009.png]]
So we need to compute $y \cdot u$ for each $u$ in $U$, as well as find the length of each $u$.

![[meta/images/Pasted image 20250331085208.png]]
$$
\frac{11}{11}u_{1} + \frac{-12}{6}u_{2} +\space \frac{-33}{\frac{33}{2}}
$$


> [!Tip] Note
> Rember that normally to solve for $\left[\mathbf{y}\right]_{\mathcal{W}}$ we would have to solve an augmented matrix. That could have many steps with many oppourtunties for roundoff errors. Having an orthogonal basis means we have an algorithm that has a simple runtime of $n$.


# Orthogonal projection:
First we wanna write a vector $\vec{y}$ in terms a vector we choose, $\vec{u}$, and another vector $\vec{z}$ orthogonal to it. Below describes what $\vec{y}$ is, as well as what $\vec{z}$

$$
\begin{align}
y = \alpha \vec{u} + z\\
\implies z=y-\alpha \vec{u}

\end{align}
$$
We need to ensure that $\vec{z}$ is orthogonal to u, which means that $z \cdot u = 0$

$$
\begin{align}
&0 = (y - \alpha u) \cdot u \\
&0=y \cdot u - (\alpha u)\cdot u \\
& 0 = y \cdot u-\alpha \left| \left| u \right| \right| 
\end{align}
$$
So we know $\vec{y}$, $\vec{u}$, but we do not know what to choose for $\alpha$, thus we solve for it:
$$
\alpha = \frac{y \cdot u}{\left| \left| u \right| \right| } 
$$
And we can use that alpha to solve for $\vec{z}$.
Note the result of $\alpha u$ is called $\hat{y}$:
![[meta/images/Pasted image 20250331091941.png]]
Which just means the orthogonal projection of  $\vec{y}$ onto the line created by
$$
\text{Span}\left\{
 \vec{u}
\right\}
$$
